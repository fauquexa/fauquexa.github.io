Formulas, assumptions and references

Geometry (quasi-static conformal mapping)

k0 = w / (w + 2s),   k1 = sinh(πw/4h) / sinh(π(w + 2s)/4h),   k′ = √(1 − k²)
εeff = 1 + ½(εr − 1) · [K(k1)/K(k1′)] · [K(k0′)/K(k0)]
Z0 = (30π / √εeff) · K(k0′)/K(k0)

K is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind, computed with the arithmetic–geometric mean, K(k) = π / (2 AGM(1, k′)), to machine precision. The line is a CPW with semi-infinite ground planes on a substrate of thickness h with vacuum below it (no backside metal), quasi-TEM, lossless and non-dispersive. Then C′ = √εeff / (c Z0) and L′ = Z0√εeff / c.

Metal thickness

Δ = (1.25t/π)[1 + ln(4πw/t)],   we = w + Δ,   se = s − Δ
εeff,t = εeff − 0.7(εeff − 1)(t/s) / [K(k0)/K(k0′) + 0.7t/s]

Z0 then uses ke = we/(we + 2se) and εeff,t. Set t = 0 to ignore thickness. For thin films (t ≪ s) the correction is about 1–2 %.

Anisotropic sapphire

A uniaxial substrate with in-plane (across the line) permittivity εx and out-of-plane εy is mapped to an isotropic one with εr = √(εxεy) and thickness h√(εx/εy). The presets use ε⊥ = 9.27 and ε∥ = 11.35 (sapphire near 4 K, Krupka et al.). Editing εr by hand switches to an isotropic custom substrate.

Kinetic inductance

Δ = 1.764 kBTc,   λ = √(ħρn / (πμ0Δ)) / √tanh(Δ/2kBT),   Ls = μ0λ coth(t/λ)

For t ≪ λ this is the familiar Ls = ħRs/(πΔ) with Rs = ρn/t (local, dirty-limit Mattis–Bardeen, valid well below Tc). The kinetic inductance per length is Lk′ = g Ls with the CPW geometric factor g = gc + gg:

gc = [π + ln(4πw/t) − k ln((1+k)/(1−k))] / [4w(1 − k²)K(k)²]
gg = k[π + ln(4π(w+2s)/t) − (1/k) ln((1+k)/(1−k))] / [4w(1 − k²)K(k)²],   k = k0

The total line has L′ = Lg′ + Lk′, Z0 = √(L′/C′), v = 1/√(L′C′) and kinetic fraction α = Lk′/L′. The geometric factor assumes t ≪ w, s.

Formulas, assumptions and references

v = 1/√(L′C′) = (c/√εeff)·√(1 − α),   f0 = v/(4ℓ) for λ/4,   f0 = v/(2ℓ) for λ/2

The kinetic inductance fraction α lowers the frequency by √(1 − α) compared with a perfect conductor. Harmonics are at 3f0, 5f0… (λ/4) and 2f0, 3f0… (λ/2), ignoring dispersion.

Near f0 both resonators behave like a parallel LC circuit with

C = C′ℓ/2,   L = 8L′ℓ/π² (λ/4) or 2L′ℓ/π² (λ/2).

Bare frequency. f0 is the frequency of an isolated resonator with an ideal open (and, for λ/4, an ideal short). The coupling capacitor, the open-end fringing field and the junction at the shorted end all pull it down. With Cc to a low-impedance feedline the estimate is f ≈ f0/√(1 + Cc/C); for a λ/2 resonator coupled at both ends, enter the sum of the two capacitances. Expect a few per cent lower frequencies in practice; simulate the final layout.

The λ/4 values C = π/(4ω0Z0) = C′ℓ/2 and L = 8L′ℓ/π² follow from the input impedance of a shorted λ/4 line; Pozar works the analogous λ/2 cases explicitly.

Formulas, assumptions and references

Saturation vapour pressure from the Magnus form with the Alduchov–Eskridge constants (pressures in hPa, temperatures in °C):

over water: ew(T) = 6.1094 exp(17.625T / (243.04 + T))   (−40 to 50 °C)
over ice: ei(T) = 6.1121 exp(22.587T / (273.86 + T))   (−80 to 0 °C)

The vapour pressure is e = RH · ew(Troom). The dew point solves ew(Td) = e and the frost point solves ei(Tf) = e:

Td = 243.04 γ / (17.625 − γ),   γ = ln(e/6.1094);   Tf = 273.86 γi / (22.587 − γi),   γi = ln(e/6.1121)

A part condenses water if 0 °C ≤ Tpart < Td and collects frost if Tpart < 0 °C and below the frost point (frost forms before dew when the dew point is below freezing). The recommended temperature is the onset temperature plus your margin. Absolute humidity uses ρv = e/(RvT) with Rv = 461.5 J kg−1 K−1. Check: 20 °C and 50 % give a dew point of 9.3 °C.

In practice: the plate thermometers lag the parts that actually see the air (radiation shields, cold fingers, the sample stage), so give a margin, vent with dry nitrogen or helium gas rather than room air, and keep the cans closed until everything is above the dew point.

Formulas, assumptions and references

Model

S21(f) = a eiα e−2πifτ [1 − (Ql/|Qc|) eiφ / (1 + 2iQl(f/f0 − 1))]

a and α describe the attenuation and phase of the wiring, τ the cable delay, and φ the rotation of the circle caused by impedance mismatch around the resonator.

Steps

  • Cable delay: a first estimate from the phase slope of the outer 10 % of points on each side, then refined by minimising the scatter of the delay-corrected data about a fitted circle.
  • Circle fit: algebraic (Taubin) fit followed by a few geometric least-squares iterations.
  • Phase fit around the circle centre: θ(f) = θ0 + 2 arctan(2Ql(1 − f/f0)), giving f0, Ql and θ0.
  • The off-resonant point P = zc + r ei(θ0 + π) gives a = |P| and α = arg P. Dividing by P normalises the circle, which then has radius r′ = Ql/2|Qc| and centre 1 − r′eiφ.
  • Diameter-correction method: 1/Qi = 1/Ql − Re(e−iφ/|Qc|) = 1/Ql − cos φ/|Qc|. The φ-corrected coupling quality factor is |Qc|/cos φ.

Uncertainties and limits

The ± values are one-standard-error estimates from the Jacobian of the full model at the fitted point, with the noise level taken from the residuals. They assume white, uncorrelated noise and are statistical only. The fit expects a single, linear (not bifurcated) resonance with a flat background, and works best when the trace covers at least a few linewidths on both sides. If the phase runs the other way through resonance (opposite sign convention), the data are complex-conjugated first; the page says so.

Example and check

The example is generated in the page from the model above with f0 = 6.0123457 GHz, Qi = 250 000, |Qc| = 120 000, φ = −0.2 rad, a = 0.0316, α = 2.0 rad, τ = 62 ns, 1001 points over ±8 linewidths, and Gaussian noise of 0.6 % of a on each quadrature (new noise each time). The true values are printed under the result so you can compare.

Formulas, assumptions and references

On resonance, input–output theory gives n̄ = (4κin/κ²) · Pin/(ħω0), where κ = ω0/Ql is the total loss rate and κin the rate at which the resonator leaks into the port you drive. With κc = ω0/Qc:

  • Notch (hanger): the resonator leaks equally in both directions along the feedline, so κin = κc/2 and n̄ = 2Ql² Pin / (Qc ħω0²). Here Qc is the one in S21 = 1 − (Ql/Qc)/(1 + 2iQlx), as fitted on the resonator-fit tab (use the φ-corrected value).
  • Reflection (one port): κin = κc, so n̄ = 4Ql² Pin / (Qc ħω0²).
  • Transmission (two ports): n̄ = 4Ql² Pin / (Qc,in ħω0²) with Qc,in the coupling Q of the input port alone (2Qc for a symmetric resonator whose total coupling Q is Qc).

The notch formula is also often written as 4Ql²Pin/(Qcħω0²); that is a factor of two higher than the input–output result for the Qc convention above, so check which definition a paper uses before comparing photon numbers. Assumes a drive exactly on resonance, a linear resonator (no bifurcation or power-dependent Q), and that Pin is the power arriving at the coupling port.

Formulas, assumptions and references

EC = e²/2CΣ,   EJ = ħIc/2e = (RQ/2RN) Δ with RQ = h/4e² ≈ 6.453 kΩ   (Ambegaokar–Baratoff, T = 0)

H = 4EC(n − ng)² − EJ cos φ is written in the charge basis |n⟩ with |n| ≤ 30, where cos φ couples n to n ± 1 with amplitude EJ/2. The tridiagonal matrix is diagonalised exactly (Sturm-sequence bisection to machine precision) at ng = 0 and 0.5.

  • f01 and f12 are the averages of their values at ng = 0 and 0.5 (the centre of each band).
  • Charge dispersion εm = Em(ng = ½) − Em(ng = 0), the full peak-to-peak width of level m, as defined by Koch et al. The qubit frequency then varies by |ε1 − ε0| peak to peak, i.e. ± |ε1 − ε0|/2 around the average; with quasiparticle parity switching, two lines appear up to |ε1 − ε0| apart.
  • Values below about 10−13 EJ are below double-precision resolution and are reported as such.

Transmon-regime asymptotics (EJ ≫ EC):

f01 ≈ √(8EJEC) − EC,   f12 − f01 ≈ −EC,   εm ≈ (−1)m EC (24m+5/m!) √(2/π) (EJ/2EC)m/2 + 3/4 e−√(8EJ/EC)

Formulas, assumptions and references

Δ = 1.764 kBTc,   IcRN = (πΔ/2e) tanh(Δ/2kBT),   EJ = ħIc/2e = Φ0Ic/2π,   LJ = Φ0/(2πIc)

Ambegaokar–Baratoff for a tunnel junction between identical BCS superconductors, using the low-temperature gap; the tanh factor is the only temperature dependence kept, so the result is valid well below Tc. LJ is the small-signal inductance at zero bias current. Measured critical currents are often somewhat below this value, so calibrate against your own junctions. RN is the room-temperature resistance corrected to low temperature (it typically rises by roughly 10–20 % on cooling; check for your process).

Formulas, assumptions and references

Δ0 = 1.764 kBTc (BCS; set another ratio for strong coupling),   Δ(T) ≈ Δ0 tanh(1.74 √(Tc/T − 1))

xqp = nqp/(2N0Δ) = √(2πkBT/Δ) e−Δ/kBT

Mattis–Bardeen conductivity (local, dirty limit), with f(E) the Fermi function:

σ1/σn = (2/ħω) ∫Δ∞ [f(E) − f(E + ħω)] g(E) dE − (1/ħω) ∫Δ−ħω−Δ [1 − 2f(E + ħω)] g(E) dE
σ2/σn = (1/ħω) ∫max(Δ−ħω, −Δ)Δ [1 − 2f(E + ħω)] (E² + Δ² + ħωE) / [√(Δ² − E²) √((E + ħω)² − Δ²)] dE
g(E) = (E² + Δ² + ħωE) / [√(E² − Δ²) √((E + ħω)² − Δ²)]

The second σ1 term only exists for ħω > 2Δ (pair breaking). With positive square roots g(E) is negative on that interval, hence the minus sign; at T = 0 this term equals the closed form (1 + 2Δ/ħω)E(k) − (4Δ/ħω)K(k), k = (ħω − 2Δ)/(ħω + 2Δ), which the page reproduces. The integrals are evaluated numerically with substitutions that remove the square-root singularities at the gap edges (E = Δ cosh u, and a cosine map on finite intervals), using Δ(T). For ħω, kBT ≪ Δ they approach

σ1/σn ≈ (4Δ/ħω) e−Δ/kBT sinh(ξ) K0(ξ),   σ2/σn ≈ (πΔ/ħω) tanh(Δ/2kBT),   ξ = ħω/2kBT

which are shown for comparison (K0 from Abramowitz and Stegun 9.8.5–9.8.6). The film’s surface impedance in the local limit is Zs = √(iωμ0/σ) coth(√(iωμ0σ) t) with σ = σ1 − iσ2; Ls = Im Zs/ω, and λ = 1/√(μ0ωσ2). Clean films (long mean free path, such as thick Al or Nb) are outside the local limit, so treat λ and Ls there as estimates.

Formulas, assumptions and references

Interdigitated capacitor

C = (N − 3) CI/2 + 2CICE/(CI + CE),   CI,E = ε0(εr + 1) ℓ K(kI,E)/K(k′I,E)
kI = sin(πη/2),   kE = 2√η/(1 + η),   η = W/(W + G)

Igreja–Dias conformal mapping for a substrate much thicker than the finger period, with air above; finger-end gaps, bus bars and nearby ground planes are ignored. The Alley/Bahl closed form C = (εr + 1) ℓ[(N − 3)A1 + A2], A1 = 4.409 × 10−6 pF/µm, A2 = 9.92 × 10−6 pF/µm (W = G, thick substrate) is shown as a cross-check; for W = G the interior terms agree to 0.4 %.

Parallel-plate capacitor

C = ε0εrA/d

No fringing fields (fine when the plate is much wider than d). Dielectric constants are typical deposited-film values and depend on the process.

Meander inductor

L = N Lself(ℓ) + 2 Σk=1N−1 (N − k)(−1)k M(ℓ, kp)
Lself = (μ0ℓ/2π)[ln(2ℓ/(w + t)) + ½ + 0.2235(w + t)/ℓ],   M = (μ0ℓ/2π)[ln(ℓ/d + √(1 + ℓ²/d²)) − √(1 + d²/ℓ²) + d/ℓ]

Partial inductances of straight strips (Grover): neighbouring strips carry opposite currents. Mutual terms treat strips as filaments, which is approximate when the gap is not much larger than the width; the short connecting segments and any ground plane are ignored (a nearby ground plane lowers L). Kinetic inductance adds Ls × (wire length / width).

Square spiral inductor

din = dout − 2nw − 2(n − 1)s,   davg = (dout + din)/2,   ρ = (dout − din)/(dout + din)
Modified Wheeler: L = 2.34 μ0n²davg/(1 + 2.75ρ)
Current sheet: L = (μ0n²davg · 1.27/2)[ln(2.07/ρ) + 0.18ρ + 0.13ρ²]

Mohan et al. report errors of a few per cent against field solvers for typical spirals without a ground plane. The kinetic term uses the wire length 4n davg. The air bridge or crossover that returns the inner end is not included.
